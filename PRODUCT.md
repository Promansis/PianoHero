# Product

## Register

product

## Users

PianoHero serves beginner to intermediate piano learners practicing at home, on either a MIDI keyboard or computer keyboard. Users are trying to turn imported songs, lessons, theory drills, and free play into repeatable practice without needing a teacher beside them. They need the app to feel immediate during play, readable while their hands are busy, and forgiving enough to keep practice moving.

## Product Purpose

PianoHero is a cross-platform piano-learning application that combines falling-note gameplay with structured piano technique, lessons, scoring, progress tracking, theory training, setup guidance, and song library management. Success means users can import music, understand what to practice next, build timing and accuracy, and return to the app because practice feels active, musical, and measurable.

## Brand Personality

Fun, immersive, educational.

The product voice should feel like a focused practice stage: energetic without becoming chaotic, instructive without sounding academic, and confident without being severe. It should encourage progress through clear feedback, visible mastery, and responsive musical interaction.

## Anti-references

PianoHero should not feel like a generic website, a school portal, a worksheet app, or a dense enterprise dashboard. Avoid page designs that look like marketing sections, classroom software, administrative forms, or overly complex UI with too many simultaneous decisions. Avoid burying the player in configuration before they can play.

## Design Principles

1. Make practice feel playable. The primary screens should foreground musical action, feedback, and momentum rather than explanation.
2. Teach through context. Lessons, setup guidance, theory, and reminders should appear where they help the next action, not as detached instructional pages.
3. Keep complexity progressive. Beginners should see a clear next step, while deeper controls should stay available when the user is ready for them.
4. Preserve the stage. Gameplay, free play, and immersive screens should feel like dedicated practice environments, not ordinary web pages.
5. Turn progress into direction. Scores, charts, streaks, and trouble spots should help the learner decide what to practice next.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No additional requirements are defined at this stage beyond the product baseline: readable contrast, keyboard-accessible controls, reduced-motion support, color-blind-friendly states, and UI text that remains legible while the user is focused on playing.
